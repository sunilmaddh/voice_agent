A final decision is consumed before acting. SMS receipt checks target and account again and never declines a later call. Caller name, number, reply body and recognized transcript are not written to structured diagnostic logs.

The service requests the declared microphone foreground-service type only for the bounded prompt/capture session and checks RECORD_AUDIO permission. Android may still deny background/locked capture; it reports that failure and retains the plain announcement and call buttons. It pauses wake capture and its owned ringtone, closes TTS before STT, and uses the existing Speech settings and system-recognition consent. The specialized incoming-call recognition entry avoids the generic engine's permanent ringtone silence and Bluetooth route takeover. Telecom retains call routing. ## Audio focus correction

**Device feedback after the correction:** the user reports `ANNOUNCEMENT_FOCUS_UNAVAILABLE_WHILE_RINGING`. The delayed focus request did not receive a usable grant within the 1.5-second ringing window. Caller announcement is therefore **not working in this tested configuration**; passing builds/tests must not be presented as a successful device fix. The precise focus owner has not been established from a device audio dump.

The current InCallService intentionally leaves ringing to Telecom: its manifest declares `IN_CALL_SERVICE_UI` but not `IN_CALL_SERVICE_RINGING`, and its call notification channel has no sound. Android documents a different integration in which the default Phone app declares `IN_CALL_SERVICE_RINGING` and assumes responsibility for incoming ringtone playback. That is a larger call-alert lifecycle change, not a permission toggle or guaranteed caller-TTS fix. It would require preserving ringtone/vibration, silence/DND, answer/end cleanup and fallback behavior and validating on the target phone before claiming support. This feedback does not justify simply extending the wait or ignoring focus denial. See [InCallService ringtone responsibility](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/telecom/InCallService).

The original implementation used ordinary assistant focus with ringtone playback attributes. A physical-device attempt reported `AUDIO_FOCUS_DENIED`; that confirms focus refusal, not its exact OS cause. The announcement now uses a dedicated `CallerAudioFocus` with matching `USAGE_NOTIFICATION_RINGTONE` attributes and transient-may-duck focus. On Android 8+ it accepts a delayed grant for at most 1.5 seconds while the call remains eligible. Timeout, cancellation and answer/end abandon the request; late callbacks cannot initiate playback. Ordinary STT/TTS focus behavior elsewhere is unchanged.

`ANNOUNCEMENT_FOCUS_DENIED_BY_ANDROID` means the request was refused; `ANNOUNCEMENT_FOCUS_UNAVAILABLE_WHILE_RINGING` means a delayed grant did not arrive in time. Neither is treated as success. This correction does not prove the device will grant ringtone focus: Telecom can lock focus, and Android 15+ also restricts focus requests by background applications. No hidden APIs, audio-mode overrides, forced SCO, ringtone silencing or playback after denial were introduced. A live incoming-call retest remains required. See [Android audio focus guidance](https://developer.android.com/media/optimize/audio-focus).
